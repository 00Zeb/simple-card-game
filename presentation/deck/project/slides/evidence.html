Built around human limitations. Which ones does an agent have?

About 75 seconds. “So does it work? The best industrial data we have is Microsoft and IBM: four teams, 40 to 90 percent lower defect density, for 15 to 35 percent more time, by their own estimate. You pay up front. In my experience, that’s what buys a predictable pace: you spend less time firefighting later, and you can ship with confidence. But the research is messy. Many studies use students on toy problems, and they disagree mostly because they’re set up differently. When one study pulled the factors apart, the short, steady rhythm mattered more than writing the test first. And honestly: who would pay to build the same software twice, with equally good teams? Nobody. Until now. With agents, I ran the same kata ten times, for a few dollars each. So here’s the question: TDD is built around human limitations. Which ones does an agent have?” Precise if asked: the defect figure is pre-release defect density, and the time figure is the teams’ own estimate. Don’t claim modular design here: these studies didn’t measure it, and part 2 shows the agents’ design didn’t change.
